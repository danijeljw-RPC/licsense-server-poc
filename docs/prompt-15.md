Read `docs/README.md` in full. This is stage 15 after verified Stripe ingestion. Inspect the billing/inbox models, licensing domain services, catalogs, customer metadata, API idempotency, email outbox, tests, and working tree. Preserve unrelated changes. Use current official Stripe documentation for event semantics and cite it; do not make licensing policy depend solely on event arrival order.

Implement configurable, explicit business policies for completed purchase, successful renewal, failed payment, cancellation-at-period-end, subscription change/deletion, refund, and dispute. Successful initial payment should create/map the customer/order/contract and issue exactly one valid license through the same issuance service used by UI/API. Successful renewal extends subscription expiry idempotently and versions/audits terms. Payment failure enters a configured grace period and sends notification; it must not silently or immediately revoke. Cancellation preserves paid-through access. Refund/dispute should flag review or apply a deliberately configured suspension rule.

Resolve Stripe price/product/customer mappings through dedicated tables and reject/quarantine unknown or conflicting mappings. Fetch current provider state through an injectable adapter when a thin, stale, or reordered event cannot establish truth. Never trust client-supplied metadata as the authoritative internal ID mapping. Every resulting license must satisfy generated ID/code, one entitlement, catalog, type/expiry, normalized customer email, and `metadata.contactEmail` invariants.

Use inbox/outbox transactions and durable idempotency so duplicate, delayed, reordered, concurrent, and replayed events cannot duplicate licenses, renewals, audit entries, or emails. Define retryable versus terminal failures and provide permission-gated operational visibility/reprocess controls without allowing arbitrary event mutation. Activation-code delivery must follow the approved one-time email policy and cannot leak through inbox/audit/logs.

Add scenario tests for every policy and malicious/edge delivery pattern, including payment recovery during grace, cancellation reversal, plan changes, same invoice through multiple events, mapping errors, worker crash boundaries, and customer email failure. Use fake Stripe and email adapters, not live services.
